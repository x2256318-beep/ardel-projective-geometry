Four-Way Comparison · English Edition

Class Field Theory, Tate Theory, Anabelian Geometry, and Ardel Projective Geometry

The three published items are three different uses of overlapping machinery, not three versions of one theory — and the fourth, Ardel projective geometry (PAG), shares their stage while differing from all three on the decisive axes. Items (1)–(3) are published literature, researched with sources. Item (4) is an unpublished framework; everything said about it is per its author's description and was not independently researched. Revised 2026-11-20 to apply the corrections from a review (see the revision record; the corrections themselves have not been independently checked).

(1) THE IDELE SIDE

The Idele Class Group Cℚ and Class Field Theory

A classification theorem: finite abelian extensions of local and global fields correspond, via the Artin reciprocity map, to open finite-index subgroups of Kv× and of the idele class group CK.

(2) THE ADELE SIDE

Harmonic Analysis on the Adele Ring 𝔸 — Tate Theory

Tate's thesis (1950): additive Fourier analysis on the locally compact ring 𝔸, Poisson summation over K ⊂ 𝔸, zeta integrals over 𝔸× — yielding analytic continuation and the functional equation of Hecke L-functions.

THEOREMANALYTIC
(3)

Anabelian Geometry

Grothendieck's programme: for hyperbolic curves, recover the scheme from its profinite, non-abelian étale fundamental group. Proved for hyperbolic curves (Nakamura, Tamagawa, Mochizuki) in the form Isomk(X, Y) ≅ IsomoutGk(π₁(X), π₁(Y)) — the Gk-compatibility is essential (over an algebraically closed field all hyperbolic curves of the same type (g, ν) have isomorphic geometric π₁). The section conjecture remains open as of the latest source checked (2023).

(4)

Ardel Projective Geometry (PAG)

A designed geometry whose points are the primes plus the infinite place of ℚ; on the cylinder Cℚ ≅ Ẑ× × ℝ>0, a Λ-cutoff carries an operator system (D = −i∂u, prime translations, inversion) and a boundary unitary U whose phases on primitive characters are fixed by postulate A1 to Tate's local-constant values (Tate-thesis additive characters, s = 1/2; the value match is outside Lean). Finite levels are the non-commutative Terwilliger algebras AN = T(0); unital, pullback-compatible, shell-preserving maps AN → AM exist only when gcd(N, M/N) = 1 (Part 258), and the system over all of divisibility uses non-unital corner maps (Parts 260–261).

The five sharp contrasts

The core of the comparison, as established by the sourced research. Status labels follow the report: theorem, definition, programme, absence-finding, judgement.

FINDING 1

Tate theory neither proves nor uses class field theory

Both theories live on Chevalley's objects — 𝔸K, 𝔸K×, CK — and in the same 1967 Cassels–Fröhlich volume, but their aims diverge: (1) classifies abelian extensions via CK; (2) does harmonic analysis on 𝔸K and CK to prove analytic properties of L-functions.

The encyclopedic summary (source 3) is explicit — a received view rather than a theorem: Iwasawa–Tate theory "does not use any deep result of class field theory" — it uses the vocabulary (a Hecke character is a quasicharacter of CK), not the reciprocity law or the existence theorem. What connects the two is Tate the person, through separate work: Tate cohomology (1952), the Tate–Nakayama theorem, and the Artin–Tate class formations (1961).

FINDING 2

Class field theory is the abelian case of anabelian geometry — literally

π₁ét(Spec 𝒪K)ab ≅ Gal(K̃/K), the narrow Hilbert class field group, canonically isomorphic to the narrow ideal class group Cl+(K) (étale covers of Spec 𝒪K do not see ramification at real places; e.g. ℚ(√3) has class number 1 but narrow class number 2, so π₁ét(Spec ℤ[√3])ab ≅ ℤ/2); with a modulus 𝔇, π₁(Spec 𝒪K, 𝔇)ab is the ray class group (Hiranouchi 2006). Abelian CFT describes the abelianizations of the arithmetic fundamental groups in idelic terms (“is exactly the computation of” is a summary, not a theorem).

The prototype, Neukirch–Uchida (1969/1976), predates the programme's naming (1983/84) by fourteen years (Neukirch 1969 assumed one of the two fields Galois over ℚ; Uchida removed this and proved functoriality), and its proofs use CFT as a tool: decomposition groups are characterized group-theoretically, then local multiplicative groups Kx× are recovered from Dxab via local class field theory, and the global K× via global class field theory.

FINDING 3

Tate theory and anabelian geometry: essentially no direct interface

No source found cites Tate (1950) as an input to, or consequence of, any anabelian theorem; the anabelian toolkit is Galois cohomology, Brauer groups, Kummer theory, and CFT — never adelic Fourier analysis. This is an absence-finding, flagged in the report as a judgement call on thin sources.

The real contacts are indirect: through (1), and through the Jacobian/Tate module — where "Tate module" is Tate's other eponym (abelian varieties, 1960s), disjoint from the 1950 thesis. In short: (2) and (3) are siblings through (1) — (2) is GL(1) analysis on the same object CK as (1) (sharing no theorems, per Finding 1); (3) is (1)'s non-abelian generalization.

FINDING 4

The theorem–postulate asymmetry is real and sharp

In Tate theory, the local functional equation is a theorem: the ratio ρ (in modern terms the γ-factor) is proved independent of the choice of Schwartz–Bruhat test function f — while ε(s, χ, ψ) depends on the additive character ψ, the Haar-measure normalization, and s, so “the value of ε” presupposes fixed conventions — and the global root number factorizes as a derived result, W(χ) = ∏v Wv(χv). Nothing about ε is assumed; ε is output.

In PAG (per the author's description), axiom A1 takes those same numerical values as an axiom about a boundary condition in a truncated geometry. Consequently A1 cannot be cited as a derivation or explanation of ε-factors — it is an encoding of them. This is a matter of logic (axiom vs. theorem), not of interpretation — provided A1 is not derived from PAG's other axioms. If A1 were shown to be the unique boundary condition satisfying an independent requirement (e.g. compatibility with inversion/reality), it would be more than an encoding. Per the author's records A1 remains a postulate; that it satisfies the conditions of Theorem 2.6 is a consistency check on root numbers, not a derivation. The conventions are fixed in the author's records: Tate-thesis additive characters (ψ∞(x) = e−2πix, ψp(x) = e+2πi{x}p), primitive χ, s = 1/2, phase value τ(χ)/(ia√N) (the Hartley kernel of Part 252).

FINDING 5

No boundary problem exists in any of the three classical items

In (1) there are no operators and no Hilbert space. In (2), analysis runs on the complete locally compact groups with Haar measure; the Fourier transform is unitary on L² of the whole group by Pontryagin duality — there is no truncated domain and hence no self-adjoint extension problem. (Tate's proof does split the ℝ>0 direction at |x| = 1, Riemann-style, and the boundary terms f̂(0), f(0) at the cut produce the poles at s = 1, 0; but this cut is a proof device: the result is independent of where one cuts, both sides are integrated out, and no boundary condition is imposed.) In (3) there is no analysis at all. Among the four, a boundary unitary gluing is constitutive only in PAG, where it arises from the Λ-cutoff of the ℝ>0 direction — for K = ℚ exactly the direction that (1) quotients away (Dℚ ≅ ℝ>0 is the kernel of the Artin map; for number fields of positive unit rank DK is larger) and that (2) integrates over completely as the Mellin/spectral parameter. Outside (1)–(3), however, cutting the scaling direction and imposing a boundary condition has precedents in the Connes lineage (1999, 2023, 2025) — see the Connes section.

The pairwise relations in detail

Three relations among the classical items. Select one to read.

Same machinery, different aim — and one person bridging both

Shared objects. Chevalley reformulated class field theory in terms of ideles, giving CK → Gal(Kab/K) with CK/NL/KCL ≅ Gal(L/K) for each finite abelian L/K, and compatible local maps Kv× → Gal(Kvab/Kv) [Milne, The Work of John Tate, §1.1].

Diverging aims. (1) is classification: the global existence theorem says every open finite-index subgroup of CK is a norm subgroup, and Gal(Kab/K) ≅ CK modulo its connected component [Encyclopedia of Mathematics, Class field theory]. (2) is analysis: Tate's thesis gives "an elegant and unified treatment of the analytic continuation and functional equation of the L-functions attached by Hecke to his Grössencharaktere" by abelian harmonic analysis on Chevalley's adeles/ideles [Kudla, Tate's Thesis]. Tate himself noted the thesis "was not a new result, except perhaps for some local aspects" — Hecke had proved the global theorem around 1920; Artin suggested reproving it by harmonic analysis on the adele ring, "treating all places of the field equally" [Tate, Abel Prize interview].

Methods. (1)'s modern method is group cohomology and class formations (or Neukirch's cohomology-free variant). (2)'s method: 𝔸K is self-dual, the discrete cocompact subgroup K ⊂ 𝔸K is its own orthogonal complement, adelic Poisson summation follows, local L-functions are integrals over Kv×, and the global functional equation follows from the local ones plus Poisson [Milne, §1.2].

Tate's double role. His contribution to (1) is not the thesis but cohomology: Tate cohomology Ĥr(G, M) for all integers r; the 1952 cup-product theorem (with r = −2 it yields the inverse of the Artin reciprocity map); the Tate–Nakayama theorem (Nakayama 1957); and the Artin–Tate class formations (1961, Ch. XIV), from which reciprocity isomorphisms are derived [Milne, §1.3]. The shared volume is literal: the thesis first appeared in print as Chapter XV of Cassels–Fröhlich (1967), pp. 305–347, alongside the cohomological CFT chapters [Kudla].

The forward feed is into Langlands. Tate's factorization W(χ) = ∏v Wv(χv) is exactly the decomposition the Langlands correspondence requires for Artin L-series; Dwork, Langlands, and Deligne built the theory of local constants on this GL(1) template, and Godement–Jacquet (1972) generalized the thesis itself to GL(n) [Milne, §1.2].

Verdict A. (1) and (2) share objects and a book, not theorems. Neither proves the other; (2) presupposes only CFT's language. Tate bridges them biographically, through two separate bodies of work.

The abelian case of the fundamental-group philosophy

The precise statement. Unramified case: π₁ét(Spec 𝒪K)ab is the Galois group of the narrow Hilbert class field, i.e. the narrow class group Cl+(K) — equal to Cl(K) only when the two coincide. For a number field K, π₁ét(Spec 𝒪K,S) is the Galois group GK,S of the maximal extension unramified outside S [Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg, Cohomology of Number Fields, 2nd ed. 2008, introduction]; its abelianization with restricted ramification is described by CFT — the narrow ideal class group in the unramified case, the ray class group with a modulus [Hiranouchi 2006]. Passing to the limit over all moduli recovers Gal(Kab/K) ≅ CK/DK.

Grothendieck's generalization. In the letter to Faltings (27 June 1983), the geometry of an anabelian scheme X is to be "completely determined by the (profinite) fundamental group π₁(X, ξ), together with" the map to π₁(K) = Gal(K̄/K); a curve is anabelian iff its Euler–Poincaré characteristic is < 0 — "in other words, when its fundamental group is not abelian", excluding types (g, ν) = (0,0), (0,1), (0,2), (1,0) [Grothendieck, Letter to Faltings]. The word "anabelian" itself marks the distance from (1): "very far from abelian groups" (Esquisse d'un programme, 1984) [Nakamura]. Reference literature lists exactly three "main generalizations of class field theory": higher CFT, the Langlands programme, and anabelian geometry.

The prototype predates the name. Neukirch (Invent. Math. 6, 1969; Caen 1976) and Uchida (J. Math. Soc. Japan 28, 1976): an isomorphism GK ≅ GL implies K ≅ L. Neukirch's original proof assumed one of K, L Galois over ℚ; Uchida removed the assumption and proved functoriality (Ikeda independently solved Neukirch's problem at about the same time, and Iwasawa remarked on the method) [Saïdi–Tamagawa; Uchida 1976, introduction]. The literature calls this anabelian geometry's "pre-history"; the proof starts from Neukirch's insight that there is a p-adic counterpart to Artin–Schreier's characterization of real closed fields, culminating in the (topological-)group-theoretic characterization of decomposition groups — the "local theory" [arXiv:2301.10342 says “topological characterization”; Saïdi–Tamagawa, “group-theoretically … using the method of Brauer groups”]. Neukirch, author of the standard CFT textbook, arrived at the first anabelian theorem from inside class field theory.

Decomposition groups are local CFT's descendants. A decomposition group D𝔭 ⊂ GK is a copy of the local absolute Galois group GK𝔭 — the very group whose abelianization local CFT computes as the profinite completion of K𝔭×. In Uchida's function-field proof, Step 2-1 recovers Kx× ⊃ 𝒪x× ⊃ Ux from Dxab explicitly by local CFT, and the global K× as a kernel in the restricted product by global CFT [Saïdi–Tamagawa, arXiv:1909.08829; Tamagawa survey].

The curve theorems. Grothendieck's conjecture (Isom form and Hom form) was proved: genus 0 by Nakamura; the affine case by Tamagawa (Compositio Math. 109, 1997); in a stronger form over sub-p-adic bases by Mochizuki (1996; local pro-p work, Invent. Math. 138, 1999). Mono-anabelian geometry (Mochizuki, Topics in Absolute Anabelian Geometry I–III, 2012–2015; Hoshi's exposition) asks for a group-theoretic algorithm reconstructing X from a single group ΠX alone. The section conjecture (1983) remains open: "to this day, this conjecture remains wide open, with only a small handful of very special examples known" [Betts, AHGT seminar, 2023]; injectivity is known, surjectivity is the open core.

Verdict B. Anabelian geometry is not outside class field theory looking in; it is CFT's generalization from within — CFT computes the abelianizations, anabelian geometry asks what the full non-abelian group remembers beyond them, and its proofs still use CFT as a lemma.

Siblings through class field theory — no direct traffic

The absence-finding. There is essentially no direct interface in the literature between Tate's thesis and anabelian geometry. No source found in the research cites Tate (1950) as an input to, or a consequence of, any anabelian theorem. The report flags this section as a judgement call on thin sources, as the research mission anticipated.

Contact via (1), at opposite ends. Both fields touch GK, but (2) uses only characters of CK — through CFT (which it does not invoke), the abelianization GKab — while (3) is defined by refusing to abelianize. The chain of contact is (2) — vocabulary — (1) — abelianization — (3).

Contact via the Jacobian — a different "Tate". For a curve X, π₁(X)ab ≅ π₁(Jac X) (geometric class field theory) [Fargues], and the abelianized fundamental exact sequence relates by pushout to the full Tate module T(AlbX) of the Albanese variety [Harari–Szamuely]. Here "Tate module" is Tate's other eponym, from abelian varieties — disjoint from the 1950 thesis. Conflating the two is a standard trap.

Contact via L-functions, at one remove. Where L-functions of curves appear (Hasse–Weil, BSD), their local factors are built from the action of decomposition/Frobenius elements on Tate modules — the groups of relation B — fed into the Artin/Hecke formalism whose GL(1) analytic theory is (2) and whose general form is Langlands.

Verdict C. (2) and (3) are siblings through (1): (2) is GL(1) analysis on the same object CK as (1), (3) is (1)'s non-abelian generalization — with no direct theorem-level traffic between them. [Judgement, based on the absence-finding above.]

Genealogy and timeline

One chronology for all four items. Note the order: the anabelian prototype (1969) predates the programme's naming (1983) by fourteen years.

1920s

Takagi and Artin

Abelian extensions classified by ray class groups; Artin's reciprocity law (1927) gives the map C𝔪/H ≅ Gal(L/K). [Milne §1.1]

1936 / 1940

Chevalley introduces ideles

"Éléments idéaux" (1936), abbreviated to "idèle" in La théorie du corps de classes, Ann. of Math. 41 (1940), 394–418 — a purely algebraic proof of CFT in which, as Chevalley noted, the theory of zeta functions "can now be omitted".

1950

Tate's thesis

Fourier analysis in number fields and Hecke's zeta-functions, Princeton PhD thesis (adviser Artin), completed May 1950. Iwasawa obtained similar results independently (1950 ICM note; 1952 letter to Dieudonné). [Milne §1.2; Kudla]

1952–1961

Cohomological class field theory

Tate cohomology (1952) and the cup-product theorem; Nakayama's generalization (1957) → Tate–Nakayama; the Artin–Tate seminar (notes circulating 1961; Benjamin 1967) axiomatizes class formations. [Milne §1.3]

1967

Cassels–Fröhlich, Algebraic Number Theory

The Brighton 1965 proceedings: cohomological CFT chapters and Tate's thesis as Chapter XV, pp. 305–347 — published there for the first time after years of influence "as a piece of clandestine literature". [Kudla]

1969 / 1976–77

Neukirch–Uchida — the prototype

Neukirch, Invent. Math. 6 (1969); Caen 1976 (Astérisque 41–42, 1977); Uchida, J. Math. Soc. Japan 28 (1976). GK ≅ GL implies K ≅ L (Neukirch 1969: one field Galois over ℚ; general case and functoriality: Uchida) — fourteen years before the programme had a name.

1983 / 1984

Grothendieck names the programme

Letter to Faltings (27 June 1983) and Esquisse d'un programme (1984): the anabelian programme is stated; the section conjecture is formulated.

1995

Bost–Connes

Hecke algebras, type III factors and phase transitions with spontaneous symmetry breaking in number theory, Selecta Math. (N.S.) 1(3) (1995), 411–456. See the placement note below.

1996–1999

Tamagawa and Mochizuki

Tamagawa, Compositio Math. 109 (1997), the affine Grothendieck conjecture; Mochizuki, J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 3 (1996) and Invent. Math. 138 (1999), the proper/sub-p-adic and local pro-p cases.

1999

Connes's trace formula

Selecta Math. (N.S.) 5 (1999); arXiv:math/9811068. The explicit formulas read as a trace formula on the adele class space, with a Λ-cutoff — one of the nearest precedents for (4)'s Λ-cutoff (see the Connes section).

1999

Neukirch, Algebraic Number Theory

Springer (German orig. 1992): Ch. IV abstract CFT, Ch. V local CFT, Ch. VI global idelic CFT — the standard textbook of item (1).

2012–2015 →

Mono-anabelian geometry

Mochizuki, Topics in Absolute Anabelian Geometry I (2012), II (2013), III (2015); Hoshi's expositions. The section conjecture is still open (status confirmed 2012 and 2023).

2021–2025

Connes–Consani (–Moscovici)

Spectral triples and ζ-cycles (arXiv 2021; Enseign. Math. 69, 2023) and Zeta Spectral Triples (Connes–Consani–Moscovici, arXiv:2511.22755, Nov 2025): rank-one perturbations of the scaling operator on [λ−1, λ] with periodic boundary conditions — formally the closest precedent for (4)'s “Λ-cutoff + boundary unitary”.

Where Bost–Connes sits

The Bost–Connes system (1995) is a descendant of (1), using (2)'s cast of characters: a C*-dynamical system with "an action of the idele class group of ℚ modulo the connected component of the identity as symmetry. The Riemann zeta function appears as the partition function"; for β > 1 the extremal KMSβ states are parametrized with free transitive action of Cℚ/Dℚ = Ẑ×, and at zero temperature the CFT isomorphism θ: Gal(ℚab/ℚ) →∼ Ẑ× "intertwines the actions of the Galois group with the action of Ẑ× by symmetries" — it realizes explicit class field theory of ℚ (Kronecker–Weber) in a quantum statistical system, with spontaneous symmetry breaking at β = 1 [Shirbisheh, arXiv:0808.1767].

It is contemporaneous with Tamagawa/Mochizuki (1996–99) but belongs to the (1)-line — operator-algebraic class field theory — not to (3). PAG's claimed lineage "Tate thesis + class field theory + Bost–Connes" (per the author's description) is therefore a lineage within the abelian/GL(1) world of (1) + (2), extended by Bost–Connes's operator-algebraic realization of (1); it does not pass through (3) at any point. Bost–Connes is not, however, a precedent for PAG's boundary unitary: the BC system has no cutoff of the ℝ>0 direction and no boundary condition. The nearer precedent for that is a different Connes line (trace formula and ζ-cycles), compared in the next section.

The Connes lineage — Λ-cutoff and boundary conditions

Items (1)–(3) have no counterpart to (4)'s boundary unitary. Outside them, however, work exists in which a cutoff of the scaling (ℝ>0) direction and a boundary condition are part of the construction. Any claim of novelty for PAG must be measured against this first. The structural comparison with Connes's framework is in the repository's docs/overview-and-comparison.en.md §5; this section adds only the cutoff/boundary point.

Precedents (each checked at abstract/introduction level only). Connes (1999), Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, Selecta Math. (N.S.) 5: the explicit formulas as a trace formula on the noncommutative adele class space, with a Λ-cutoff and zeros read as an absorption spectrum. Connes–Consani (2023), Spectral triples and ζ-cycles, Enseign. Math. 69, 93–148: the canonical spectral triple of a circle of length L conditioned by eigenvectors (built from prolate spheroidal functions) of the Weil quadratic form. Connes–Consani–Moscovici (2025), Zeta Spectral Triples, arXiv:2511.22755: self-adjoint operators as rank-one perturbations of the scaling operator on [λ−1, λ] with periodic boundary conditions.

Overlap. Cutting the ℝ>0 direction to a finite interval and building the end-gluing (boundary condition) into the operator is formally the same type as (4)'s Λ-cutoff and boundary unitary U. “Cut and glue at the boundary” therefore cannot by itself be claimed as PAG's novelty [judgement].

Differences (per the author's description). (a) PAG fixes the phases of the boundary unitary on primitive characters by postulate A1 to Tate's local-constant values; the abstracts/introductions above show no element that postulates ε-factors as boundary phases (full texts not checked; confidence low–medium). (b) Aims differ: the Connes lineage seeks a spectral realization bearing on the Riemann hypothesis; PAG does not take that direction, and its monograph records the negative result that attempts to approach the zeros ended in injection or circularity (PAG is not an approach to the Riemann hypothesis). (c) PAG's finite levels are the non-commutative Terwilliger algebras AN, a different object from the Connes lineage's finite-dimensional approximations (prolate functions, Sonin spaces).

Open question: the relation between (4)'s compatibility classification of boundary unitaries (Theorem 2.6) and CCM 2025's periodic boundary condition plus rank-one perturbation. Not checked against the full texts.

What could not be verified — stated plainly

  • The decomposition Cℚ ≅ Ẑ× × ℝ>0 from a primary textbook page. It is standard, and is used identically by all Bost–Connes sources (Cℚ/Dℚ = Ẑ×, Dℚ ≅ ℝ>0) and stated as a lemma in one weak (Zenodo) preprint; the research did not open a primary textbook page stating the product decomposition verbatim. Confidence remains high, but the citation is indirect.
  • Relation C is an absence-finding. No source explicitly asserts "(2) and (3) do not interact" — absence cannot be sourced directly. It rests on the toolkits visible in the anabelian surveys (CFT, Brauer groups, Kummer theory — never adelic Fourier analysis) and is flagged as a judgement.
  • The archimedean place in (3) rests on survey-level reading plus the Artin–Schreier remark in arXiv:2301.10342; no single source states "the infinite place is absent in anabelian geometry" in those words.
  • Neukirch's personal path from CFT to the 1969 theorem is documented only at the level of the theorem's content and proof technique, plus secondary remarks; no autobiographical source was found.
