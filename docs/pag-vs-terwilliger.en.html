Comparative Research — survey of 2026-11-20

Ardel Projective Geometry
and the Terwilliger Algebra

The conclusion has two layers. At the finite level, the algebra AN of Ardel projective geometry (PAG) is the Terwilliger algebra T(0): the definitions coincide, and the single-level structure (Wedderburn decomposition and dimension for prime powers, tensor decomposition for coprime moduli) is known (BST 2010, Hanaki–Kim–Maekawa 2011, Bastian–Humphries 2026). Of what PAG builds on top, the level-to-level maps are known on the existence side and on the E-side of the corner maps, and the directed system is a [small re-derivation]; the only remaining novelty candidates are the non-existence side for unital maps and the generation classification for composite N (both at low–medium confidence, with MathSciNet/zbMATH checks still outstanding). The monograph layer (Tate's γ-factors, self-adjoint extensions) has no corresponding problem setting in Terwilliger theory. Conversely, the general theory — arbitrary schemes, Leonard pairs — extends far beyond PAG's restriction to ℤ/N.

Three points first

What is identical

AN coincides, by checking definitions, with the Terwilliger algebra T(0) of the orbit Schur ring (orbit S-ring) on ℤ/N whose basic sets are the gcd shells. PAG's name “shell algebra” is another name for this identification.

Where the differences concentrate

“How to connect the algebras for different N” and “what to connect the algebra to.” Of the former, only the non-existence side for unital maps is not known (novelty candidate, low–medium confidence). The latter (the monograph layer) lies outside Terwilliger theory. On single-level structure, by contrast, the literature is far broader, and PAG's prime-power and tensor results are special cases of it.

⚠ Terminology trap: in the literature, “cyclotomic scheme” usually means the scheme on a finite field Fq defined by cosets, which is a different object from the gcd-shell scheme on ℤ/N. Confusing the two would wrongly transfer finite-field results (Jacobi sums and the like) to PAG. The standard name for PAG's object is the scheme coming from the “orbit Schur ring over a cyclic group (cyclotomic S-ring).”

01The identification — AN = T(0)

PAG's finite-level algebra AN is generated inside End(ℂℤ/N) by two kinds of operators.

Dt ≡ E*t : diagonal projection onto the shell { x ∈ ℤ/N : gcd(x, N) = t } (t | N) = dual idempotent at base point 0
Et : projection onto the frequency-side shell (functions whose Fourier transform is supported on the shell) = primitive idempotent of the Bose–Mesner algebra

Et is a primitive idempotent of the Bose–Mesner algebra of the association scheme whose relations are separated by gcd. Dt is the dual idempotent E*t with respect to base point 0 — exactly the diagonal projection onto the point-side shell (the pre-revision version called this projection Et). This pair of generator families is precisely the definition of the Terwilliger algebra.

Definition (Terwilliger, 1992) [V]

For a commutative association scheme Y and a vertex x, introduce the non-commutative, associative, semisimple ℂ-algebra T = T(x), called the subconstituent algebra. In modern formulation it is “the subalgebra of the matrix algebra generated by the Bose–Mesner algebra and the dual idempotents with respect to x” (confirmed from the abstract of Maleki, arXiv:2310.19609).

Sources: Terwilliger, “The subconstituent algebra of an association scheme” I–III, J. Algebraic Combin. 1 (1992), 2 (1993). / Survey by Herman, Indian J. Pure Appl. Math. 52 (2021) 631–642 [S]

So the difference is not “whether AN is a Terwilliger algebra.” It lies in which scheme's T it is, and what is built on top of T.

PAG's scheme is special. It is the orbit Schur ring under the full automorphism group (ℤ/N)×, whose basic sets are exactly the gcd shells. It has only as many classes as N has divisors, so its Bose–Mesner algebra is small. This is why the result “for group schemes T is the full matrix algebra” (below) does not transfer to PAG.

02What the Terwilliger algebra is — the standard theory

The Terwilliger algebra (subconstituent algebra), introduced by Paul Terwilliger in 1992, is an algebra reflecting the combinatorial structure of an association scheme. Its generators are the adjacency matrices Ai (or their primitive idempotents Ei) and the dual idempotents E*i(x), diagonal matrices determined by a base point x.

T(x) = ⟨ { Ai } ∪ { E*i(x) } ⟩ ⊆ M|X|(ℂ),   (E*i(x))uu = (Ai)x,u

Central themes

  • Irreducible modules and thinness: decompose the standard module V = ℂX into irreducible T-modules. dim E*iW ≤ 1 is called thin, dim EiW ≤ 1 dual thin. Classifying modules by invariants such as endpoint and diameter is the backbone of Parts I–III [V].
  • Wedderburn decomposition: T is closed under conjugate transpose and semisimple, so T ≅ ⊕ Matni(ℂ). Determining the components, multiplicities and dimension scheme by scheme is the standard “structure problem.”
  • P-/Q-polynomial schemes: linked to Delsarte theory (1973, coding theory); the Bose–Mesner linear-programming bound is refined to a semidefinite bound via the Terwilliger algebra (Schrijver, Tanaka) [S].
  • Leonard pairs and systems: pairs of operators, each diagonal in a basis in which the other is irreducible tridiagonal. Irreducible T-modules that are thin and dual thin coincide with Leonard systems, classified by the Askey scheme / q-Racah polynomials (Terwilliger, arXiv:math/0306301, 2003) [V].
  • Tridiagonal pairs, q-Onsager, quantum groups: a large representation-theoretic layer leading to tridiagonal algebras, (q-)Onsager algebras and representations of Uq(ŝl2) [S].
  • Almost commutativity: the property that every non-primary irreducible module is one-dimensional. Tanaka (2010; J. Algebraic Combin. 33 (2011) 1–10) gave equivalent conditions, one being “a wreath product of one-class schemes and finite abelian group schemes” [S]. This concept leads directly to Bastian–Humphries in §3.

Reduction to two generators is guaranteed in the P- and Q-polynomial case. (This does not rule out T = ⟨A, A*⟩ for other schemes.) For distance-regular graphs / P- and Q-polynomial schemes, M is generated by the single adjacency matrix A = A1 and M* by A* = A*1(x), so T = ⟨A, A*⟩ [S]. This is a different matter from §5's “generation by subsets of the generators.”

Main references: Bannai–Ito, Algebraic Combinatorics I (1984) / Brouwer–Cohen–Neumaier, Distance-Regular Graphs (1989) / Herman survey (2021).

03Cyclotomic / gcd schemes — what the literature actually treats

This is where confusion is easiest. Several objects have similar names.

① Finite-field cyclotomic scheme Cyc(q, N)

Uses the cosets Ci of a subgroup of Fq×, with relations x − y ∈ Ci. This is usually what “cyclotomic scheme” means in the literature. Ishibashi–Ito–Yamada (1999) analyzed the representations of T via Jacobi sums and determined the irreducible representations completely in the class-number-2 case [S].

② Group association scheme

Relations by conjugacy class. If G is abelian the classes are singletons, dim T0 = |G|2, i.e. T equals the full matrix algebra M|G|(ℂ) (Bannai–Munemasa, Kyushu J. Math. 49 (1995) 93–102) [V]. This is not the fusion into gcd shells, and the conclusion does not transfer to AN.

③ Orbit Schur ring over a cyclic group — PAG's object

The S-ring obtained by quotienting by the orbits of a subgroup of the automorphism group. When the subgroup is all of Aut(ℤ/N) = (ℤ/N)×, the orbits are exactly the gcd shells. This is the family to which PAG's AN belongs.

④ Wreath and direct products of one-class schemes — the same as ③

For N = pk, vp(x − y) = j ⇔ “the first p-adic digit (from the bottom) at which x and y differ is j”, so the scheme of ③ is isomorphic to the k-fold wreath product Kp ≀ ⋯ ≀ Kp. For general N, by the Chinese remainder theorem, it is the direct product Πp Kp≀vp(N) of the prime-power factors. Hence the results of Bhattacharyya–Song–Tanaka (2010) apply directly to AN for N a prime power (the pre-revision statement “the construction differs, so the formula cannot be read as AN's” was wrong).

The closest 2026 result — Bastian–Humphries

Bastian–Humphries, “Schur rings over cyclic groups having Almost Commutative Terwilliger algebras”, arXiv:2606.19095 (submitted 17 June 2026) [V] abstract checked

Classifies the orbit Schur rings over cyclic groups that give almost commutative Terwilliger algebras. According to the abstract, the subgroup of automorphisms must be trivial, or the full automorphism group when the order of the cyclic group is a prime power. For order 2n these are the only two; for pn with p an odd prime, a subgroup of order pn−1 is also allowed. For orders that are not prime powers, only the orbit ring of the trivial subgroup is almost commutative. It also treats wedge products and determines completely when a Schur ring over a cyclic group gives an almost commutative T.

Reading for PAG (an interpretation, not a quotation): PAG's ring is the orbit ring of the full automorphism group, so by this theorem AN is almost commutative exactly when N is a prime power; if N has two or more distinct prime factors, it is not. This gives a literature anchor for the split between prime powers and composite numbers that PAG has encountered. The repository's dictionary (records/audit256/dictionary_before_part256.md) records that the paper's body contains the prime-power dimension formula ((3k²+5k+2)/2 for p odd, (3k²+3k+2)/2 for p = 2) and a Thm 5.2 citing Hanaki–Kim–Maekawa (T(A × B) ≅ T(A) ⊗ T(B)) (partial check of the body; this page's survey saw only the abstract). The reading was also checked numerically: all 11 prime powers (≤ 32) are almost commutative, and all 7 non-prime-powers (6 ≤ N ≤ 30) are not (records/terwilliger-check/; the dictionary records 45/45 up to 128 and 34/34 for 6 ≤ N ≤ 60).

Known The dimension and Wedderburn decomposition of T(0) for the gcd / orbit scheme follow from known results via the isomorphism above. For prime powers, BST gives T(Kp≀k) ≅ Mk+1(ℂ) ⊕ ℂk(k+1)/2 − b (b = k for p = 2, 0 otherwise), so dim Apk = (3k²+5k+2)/2 (p odd), (3k²+3k+2)/2 (p = 2). For general N, the tensor product theorem for direct products (below) gives dim AN = Πp dim Apkp. Numerics: the dimension formula matches for 11 prime powers (the dictionary records 44/44 up to 128), and the dimension is multiplicative for 6 coprime pairs (the pre-revision “not found” was wrong).

For composite N, the Chinese remainder theorem makes ℤ/N a direct product of its prime-power parts, and the orbit scheme under the full unit group is exactly the direct product scheme of the prime-power gcd schemes (gcd(x, ab) = gcd(x, a)·gcd(x, b)). Hence HKM Thm 3.1 and BST give dim AN and the Wedderburn decomposition for general N. I have not seen a paper that states this application explicitly, but it is a consequence in a few lines Known consequence.

The tensor product theorem for direct product schemes, T(X×Y) ≅ T(X) ⊗ T(Y), is HKM Thm 3.1 and can also be shown elementarily: the relation matrices Ai⊗Bj and dual idempotents E*i⊗F*j of the direct product lie in T(X)⊗T(Y); conversely Ai⊗I = Σj Ai⊗Bj and E*i⊗I generate T(X)⊗1, and likewise for 1⊗T(Y). The Hamming scheme's T is a symmetric tensor power because the Hamming scheme is a fusion obtained by symmetrizing the relations of the direct product; it is not a counterexample to this theorem (the pre-revision “not found” was wrong).

Related: Bastian's thesis “Terwilliger Algebras for Several Finite Groups” (BYU, 2021) treats the Wedderburn decompositions of group schemes of finite abelian and dihedral groups [S].

04Level-to-level maps and limits — the largest difference

Not found No paper treating homomorphisms between Terwilliger algebras of different schemes as a general theory was found in the sources surveyed. The individual results on PAG's level-to-level maps, however, differ in status as follows (labels per the repository's assessments).

  • Existence side for unital maps (when gcd(N, M/N) = 1; Part 258, PAG262): follows from the tensor product theorem for direct products (HKM) Known
  • Non-existence side for unital maps (when a prime p divides both N and M/N, there is no unital map that is compatible with pullback and does not mix shells): Novelty candidate (small), confidence low–medium. Not found in a blind question test or in the builder's text checks (Bastian–Humphries, Watanabe, Kim). Not to be used externally as novel until MathSciNet/zbMATH and the HKM text have been checked (CLAUDE.md §3).
  • Non-unital corner maps for all N | M (Part 260, PAG264): the E-side is the operator version of corners by idempotents of quotient schemes Known; the rest is a [small re-derivation]. Nearby structures: corner algebras of wreath products (non-unital subalgebras) and non-unital homomorphisms in another setting (generalized Terwilliger algebras of hypercubes, arXiv:2301.08366) (per the repository's records).
  • Directed system and limit over the divisibility order (Part 261, PAG265): [small re-derivation]. The local-unit statement is general nonsense for direct limits of unital stages with multiplicative transition maps; the only content carried is the absence of a left unit.

So, of PAG's “unital vs corner map” dichotomy, only the non-existence side can be said to be not known — and that too is a judgement within the range checked.

Adjacent literature that is not the same thing

Categories of schemes and functoriality — up to Bose–Mesner

In Hanaki's category of association schemes AS / AS₀ (pointed), monomorphisms and epimorphisms are characterized; kernels are subschemes and cokernels quotient schemes [S]. French, “Functors from Association Schemes”, arXiv:1208.1173, gives a broad subcategory of admissible morphisms on which “the standard construction sending a scheme to an algebra (the adjacency algebra)” is functorial. In Kuribayashi–Matsuo's summary, French's Corollary 6.6 says that “scheme ↦ Bose–Mesner algebra” is a functor [S]. No extension to T(x) was found — a natural sticking point, since T depends on a base point and dual idempotents that morphisms of schemes need not preserve.

Product theorems are not level-to-level maps

The direct/wreath product results of §3 describe the T of a single product scheme in terms of the factors' data; they are not homomorphisms T(smaller level) → T(larger level) and involve no pullback on ℂX.

Multi-base-point variants also stay inside one scheme

Suzuki's T with respect to a vertex set, and Marashdeh's two-base-point version T(a,b) = ⟨T(a), T(b)⟩ (arXiv:2609.00462, prime-order circulant/Paley graphs) [S], add generators inside a single Mp(ℂ); they are not maps between different schemes.

05Generation classification — when do subsets of the generators generate everything?

Not found For the cyclotomic / gcd scheme on ℤ/N, no paper classifying which subsets of {Et} ∪ {E*t} generate all of T(0) was found in the sources surveyed. The status is split, however: the prime-power case and ⟨M*, EN⟩ = AN ⇔ N ∈ {1, 4} ∪ primes follow in a few lines from the structure theorem and the dimension formula Known consequence. The only remaining novelty candidates are, for composite N, item 66 (the condition for the sum of two dual algebras) and items 67–69 (the complete classification): assessed as “at the level of a small note,” confidence medium, MathSciNet/zbMATH checks not done (candidate 1 of CLAUDE.md §3).

The literature has only two generation themes, of a different character:

  • Coarse reduction to two generators (§2): in P- and Q-polynomial schemes every Ai is a polynomial in A1 and every A*i a polynomial in A*1, so the generating set collapses to ⟨A, A*⟩. This is polynomial generation inside M and M*, not a classification of subsets of idempotents. Moreover the gcd scheme is in general not P- and Q-polynomial in that sense, so this does not answer PAG's question.
  • Generation by triple products: in connection with triple regularity, T is generated by the span of triple products E*i Aj E*k (T₀ of Bannai–Munemasa / Bhattacharyya–Song–Tanaka) [S]. This too is not a classification of generator subsets.

06Outward connections — item by item

Results of checking, in five items, whether PAG's monograph layer and character-theoretic content connect to the Terwilliger literature.

Implication: PAG's monograph layer — D = −i∂u on the idele-class cylinder Ẑ× × ℝ>0, the boundary unitary U of self-adjoint extensions, and the gluing to Tate's γ-factors by axiom A1 — cannot be described as a Terwilliger algebra, because the theory of T has no corresponding problem setting. Within PAG, the overlap with Terwilliger theory stops at the layer of the finite-level algebras AN; above that, the constructions are PAG's own.

07The other direction — what the general theory has and a ℤ/N-only theory lacks

The difference is not one-way. The general Terwilliger theory has a breadth that a theory of schemes on ℤ/N alone cannot reach.

  • Arbitrary association schemes: schemes from designs, finite geometries, permutation groups, Latin squares, regular polyhedra, and more. Example: the dodecahedron's T ≅ M₆(ℂ) ⊕ M₆(ℂ) ⊕ M₂(ℂ) (Levstein–Penazzi; also a counterexample to a conjecture of Terwilliger) [S]. Any finite set X and any base point x are allowed; when the scheme is not vertex-transitive, T(x) genuinely depends on x — a phenomenon invisible in a highly symmetric setting like base point 0 in ℤ/N.
  • Non-commutative schemes and arbitrary coefficients: Hanaki defined T for arbitrary (not necessarily commutative) schemes over any commutative unital ring. In the modular version (“Modular Terwilliger algebras”, arXiv:2004.09692 / Graphs Combin. 37 (2021)), T can fail to be semisimple in positive characteristic (when the characteristic divides a valency); for the Johnson scheme J(n,2), dim T is 16 or 15 depending on the characteristic [S].
  • Families of distance-regular graphs with unbounded diameter: Hamming schemes H(d,q) (T a symmetric tensor power; for the hypercube QD, T is a homomorphic image of U(sl₂(ℂ)), Go, Eur. J. Combin. 23 (2002)), Johnson schemes J(v,d), Grassmann, dual polar spaces, Doob schemes (direct products of Shrikhande graphs and K₄; Tanabe, J. Algebraic Combin. 6 (1997) 173–195 [V]), Odd graphs, distance-biregular graphs, generalized quadrangles, trees. None of these are schemes on ℤ/N.
  • Group schemes of non-abelian groups: conjugacy-class schemes of arbitrary finite groups G (Bannai–Munemasa's dimension bounds; Balmaceda–Oura for S₅ and A₅; Maleki for Cn ⋊ C₂; Bastian–Humphries's Camina/Frobenius classification). The base-point theory at the identity has no counterpart on ℤ/N [V/S].
  • Abstract module theory beyond schemes: the whole representation-theoretic edifice of Leonard pairs/systems, tridiagonal pairs and tridiagonal algebras, (q-)Onsager algebras, Uq(sl₂) and affine quantum groups, and Askey–Wilson polynomials (§2). The ℤ/N family sees only a thin slice of it.
  • Applications outside pure structure: Delsarte's LP/SDP bounds for codes and designs, Assmus–Mattson-type theorems (Tanaka 2009), spin models and type-II matrices, quantum walks and entanglement on Hamming/Johnson graphs (Vinet et al.) [S].

Balance: PAG's single-level structure (prime powers, tensor products) is a special case of the general theory. What was not found as a special case within the general theory is the non-existence side for unital level-to-level maps (§4), the generation classification for composite N (§5), and the themes of the monograph layer (§6). The first two are novelty candidates (low–medium confidence, checks incomplete); the last means Terwilliger theory has no problem setting for them. The two are not related by inclusion; they are extensions in different directions sharing one algebra AN = T(0).

08Check against the dictionary — the attributions stand

The pre-revision version said in this section that “the [known] attributions in the dictionary for Parts 225–226 (prime-power Wedderburn/dimension) and Part 228 (tensor decomposition) may be less direct than written.” That warning is withdrawn, for these reasons.

Parts 225–226: the gcd scheme on ℤ/pk is isomorphic to Kp≀k, so these are exactly the results of BST (2010). The dimension formula also agrees with that of Bastian–Humphries (2026) (dictionary records: 44/44 up to 128; this page's recomputation also matches for 11 prime powers).

Part 228: for coprime a, b the scheme on ℤ/ab is a direct product scheme, and T(X×Y) ≅ T(X)⊗T(Y) is HKM Thm 3.1 (the dictionary records checking the theorem statement in the body), and can also be shown elementarily. Numerics: dim Aab = dim Aa·dim Ab for 6 coprime pairs.

The dictionary's [known] labels therefore stand as they are.

Unchecked items (left open)

  • The HKM body: the dictionary says “Thm 3.1 checked in the authors' PDF,” while CLAUDE.md §3 (candidate 3) says “the body could not be retrieved from the builder's environment.” Which session checked it needs to be recorded consistently. Since the tensor product theorem itself is backed by an elementary argument, the conclusion on attribution does not change.
  • The body of Bastian–Humphries, arXiv:2606.19095: the presence of the dimension formula and Thm 5.2 rests on the dictionary's records (partial check of the body). This page's survey saw only the abstract, and arXiv could not be opened directly from this environment.
  • Ishibashi–Ito–Yamada (1999) was checked via an Oberwolfach report and summaries in citing papers; the body was not read (the bibliographic data itself was confirmed).
  • The possibility cannot be ruled out that a new 2025–2026 preprint not indexed under search terms like “homomorphism / inductive limit / pullback / corner + Terwilliger” treats level-to-level maps. Every “not found” is limited to the sources surveyed.

Method and limits of this survey

This page is based on a literature survey carried out on 20 November 2026 (including checks of source text on publisher/arXiv pages). Items marked “not found” mean that targeted searches (including combinations of homomorphism, inductive limit, pullback, corner, etc. with Terwilliger) turned up no relevant paper; they are not claims of mathematical non-existence. The bodies of Hanaki–Kim–Maekawa (2011) and Ishibashi–Ito–Yamada (1999) were not read in this survey, and statements about them are at summary level [S] (for HKM Thm 3.1, see the repository dictionary's record). For Bastian–Humphries (2026) the abstract was checked, and implications for PAG are written as a “reading (interpretation),” kept separate from quotation.
