## 0. Boot procedure at the start of a session

Before starting work on this project in a new session, do the following in order.

1. **Re-read the definitions**: Re-read the author's definitions in §1 as they are. In particular, confirm that "the foundation is discrete, and continuous objects do not exist in the foundation" and that "the parameter that determines the resolution is undecided (do not identify it with $N$, $K$ or $\Lambda$)". Do not rewrite the definitions or interpret them expansively.
2. **Confirm the current position**: Using §2 and the latest handoff memo, confirm the completed Parts, the counts, and the unfinished questions of the original path (continuation of B-3, the structure theorem, Theorem A on $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, the formulation of resolution and emergence).
3. **Mapping + residue**: When dealing with a new object, mapping it to existing mathematics (the dictionary check and the blind naming test of §7) is presupposed; mapping is an obligation, not a prohibition. At the same time, impose as a completion condition of the boot that "the question remaining after the mapping can be stated in the language of geometry, the global, and limits". If it cannot be stated, treat that object as a device for local computation and do not start a leading Part on it.
4. **Handshake (operation check)**: Before starting work, state the current situation in your own words, divided into three: (a) what has been formalized, (b) the author's interpretive layer (the definitions and design philosophy of §1), (c) the unfinished global questions. If you write a sentence that mixes the three, the boot is incomplete and must be redone.

The purpose of this procedure is not to avoid mapping to existing mathematics. The identification with the Terwilliger algebra was itself progress; the failure was that the mapping came about 40 Parts late. What must be prevented is not mapping, but thinking stopping inside the finite structure that one has mapped to.

## 1. The purpose of the project (the original path)

- The purpose is the construction of "Ardel projective geometry". "Ardel" is the author's coinage abbreviating idele-adele, and denotes a geometry containing the multiplicative ideles and the additive adeles. PAG stands for Projective Adelic Geometry.
- The author's definitions (treat as settled; do not redefine or reinterpret):
  - The points are the primes plus the infinite place. At the infinite place, the infinitely large and the infinitely small overlap. The reading of this "overlap" was decided by the author on 2026-11-12: **read it as a twisted gluing (boundary unitary $U$)**. The reading of the identification of the two ends as a group quotient is not adopted because it is incompatible with A1 (the cubic character modulo 9 is a counterexample). The quotient by $W$ is also compatible with A1 but was not adopted ($D$ does not descend to the quotient).
  - The foundation is discrete, and continuous objects such as lines do not exist in the foundation.
  - A line is an effective appearance that emerges when the resolution is lowered.
  - However, the parameter that determines the resolution is **undecided**. Do not arbitrarily identify it with any of $N$, $K$ or $\Lambda$.
  - Placement of Proposition 3 (2026-11-13; recorded as a decision of the author-side Muse, to whom the choice was delegated by the author; the author can overturn it at any time): the wording of the definitions above is not changed. The mathematical place of the operation of resolution is put in the interpretive layer, not as "a transition within the object (a change from discrete to continuous)" but as **a choice of a way of cutting (a view)** of the global object. That is, the object is the whole in which the places coexist in parallel from the start as the factors of a restricted product; the finite-level quotients and the $\Lambda$-cutoff (the two axes separated in A8) are views of that whole; and the question of emergence is read as "how does a line look when the view is made coarser". The prohibition on identifying the resolution with $N$ or $\Lambda$ is maintained as before (what was decided is where the question is placed, not the parameter). Later, on 2026-11-14, the original text of the author's 08:41 definition reached the author side through the construction side's handoff memo (`PAG2_handoff_part257.md`): "Escaping into the completion (limit) and sliding into the continuum is what I call the resolution dropping. I do not wish for the continuum to be observed; I maintain the observation of the discrete structure even in the limit." This definition does not contradict the placement above — the operation on the side where the resolution drops is sliding into the completion, the stance is maintaining the observation of the discrete structure even in the limit, and the limit is taken on the side of the direct limit (the side where all places are measured at once). When using completions ($L^2$, $\mathbb{R}$, the product topology), state as a reading that they are "read as operations on the side where the resolution drops". Do not write the continuum appearing in closures or completions as the "effective appearance" or as a goal. Also, on 2026-11-13 at 08:38 the author adopted, on the construction side, L1 (resolution = the set of places measured) as the reading of Proposition 3 (L1 is a proposal adopted as a reading, not the wording of the definition itself; it is not identified with any of $N$, $K$ or $\Lambda$). By the author's instruction (2026-11-12), the expression 「直線の創発」 ("emergence of the line") is not used (when touching on Proposition 3, refer to it as "Proposition 3 of §1 (resolution and effective appearance)" and write in mathematical language what changes and how).
  - Reading of "projection" (2026-11-18, adopted with reservations by the author; interpretive layer, not a formalized theorem): the meaning of the name is "fusing adelic geometry and idelic geometry by projection" (2026-11-16, the author). The "projection" in the name refers to the pair of a stage and a transform, with the transform as the main sense. In the author's words: "the stage is P¹, and the composition of gluings acting on it is called the projection". The stage is the projective line P¹ (inversion swaps 0 and ∞; at the finite level, Parts 19–28 (PAG23–PAG32)). ## 3. Settled matters concerning the Terwilliger algebra (2026-11-07, the author's definition check)

- The shell algebra $A_N$ is **exactly the Terwilliger algebra $T(0)$** of the commutative association scheme coming from the cyclotomic S-ring (orbit Schur ring) on $\mathbb Z/N$. Dictionary: $D_t$ = the dual idempotent $E_t^*$ with respect to the base point $0$; $E_t$ = a primitive idempotent of the Bose–Mesner algebra; $A_N = \langle D_s, E_s\rangle = \langle M, M^*\rangle$. Details in `terwilliger-dictionary.md`.
- Therefore treat the following as **known** and do not write them as new:
  - The Wedderburn decomposition and dimension formula for prime powers (Parts 225–226) = the results of Bhattacharyya–Song–Tanaka (2010) on lexicographic powers; agrees with the dimension formula of Bastian–Humphries (2026).

## 7. Convergence alarm (detection of convergence to existing knowledge)

Background: the finite-level part of PAG 2.0 rebuilt the structure theory of the shell algebra $A_N$ over about 40 Parts without noticing that it is exactly the Terwilliger algebra $T(0)$. Detect convergence by signals, not by results. The following signals and responses are operating rules.

### Signals (report if even one appears)

1. **Giving your own name to an object constructible by a standard definition**: objects defined by universality, duality, generation, center, commutant, etc. almost certainly have names in the literature. When introducing a new object, before starting to prove structure theorems, search for candidate literature names and report the candidates and the search range.
2. **The questions are moving to "structure, classification, dimension"**: if questions of the type "what is the center, what is the dimension, when is it everything" continue for three Parts, propose an audit as a sign of having entered the interior of an existing field.
3. **Literature checks continue not to be carried out**: if ten Parts in a row have no record of carrying out a literature check (name search, dictionary check), report it as a yellow signal. (Changed in the calibration of 2026-11-08: since it turned out that whether there are citations is not recorded in the design memos and does not function as a proxy indicator, the condition was changed from "number of citations" to "record of carrying out a check".)
4. **Proofs keep advancing only by combinations of elementary components**: treat the continuation of a state that needs no new idea itself as a sign of walking in known territory.

### Mechanisms (always in operation)

- **Maintaining the dictionary**: maintain, as a living document, a correspondence table between our own terms (left column) and literature terms (right column) (`terwilliger-dictionary.md` is its template). When five rows with an empty right column accumulate, do not start a new Part; first propose an investigation to fill in the right column.
- **Blind naming test**: for a newly introduced standard object, give only the definition, with our own names replaced by neutral symbols, to a session that does not share the construction context, and have it list candidate literature names. Check the returned candidates against actual literature before recording them in the dictionary. (Effectiveness confirmed by a retrospective measurement on 2026-11-08: the Terwilliger algebra $T(0)$ of the gcd scheme was identified from the definition of `shellAlg`.)
- **Blind question test** (adopted 2026-11-12): before starting on a new question, give only the definitions and the statement of that question to a session that does not share the context, and ask "is this question an existing result? Which literature has the answer?" This is the question version of the naming test, moving detection from after the fact (audits after closing a Part) to before. Reflect the returned answer in the pre-registration of §4 (making the residue explicit).
- **Conditions for closing a Part**: each Part's note must carry a [known / re-derived / new candidate] label for every main result. For new candidates, attach a search log (search terms, where searched, range), and state explicitly that "not found" is not a proof of non-existence.
- **Stopping rule for the tail of checks** (2026-11-20, the author's decision): the iteration of re-checking fixes made after a check may be stopped, with the remaining fixes put on the integrated check of the next Part and the Part closed, only when the last check returns only low or very-low findings and there is neither a sentence added that strengthens a claim nor a rewriting of a record of the author's decisions. If there is a fix that changes a claim, one more integrated check is done, and after that the Part is closed under this rule. Closure of the ledger is declared only on the basis of a check whose input includes the fixes themselves (the lesson of 2026-11-19). Records of decisions are not rewritten; the breakdown of the grounds is added as a later note.
- **Restatement test**: every 10–20 Parts, carry out an audit asking "restate the results so far using only the language of existing fields". If the restatement succeeds, report it to the author. Note the interpretation, however (corrected in the re-audit of 2026-11-12): being restatable shows only that "no new concepts were needed", and is not treated as evidence that "there is no new content". Since every theorem written in Lean can by definition be restated, this test alone has weak discriminating power.
- **Control-group test** (adopted 2026-11-12): for statements claiming arithmetic or global content, check them against control groups together with the restatement test — does the same statement hold for the function field $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ (the image of the norm is $q^{\mathbb Z}$, and no continuous line appears) and for Beurling generalized primes? A statement that also holds for the control groups is treated as using only form and density, not arithmetic specific to $\mathbb{Q}$. In particular, treat statements that hold only by the density of the logarithms of the primes as having no arithmetic content.
- **Origin of the question**: at the beginning of a new Part, state which unfinished question of the original path (§1, §4) it serves. Do not start a Part whose origin cannot be written.
- At each Part's completion, report to the author the number of unfilled dictionary entries and "the number of consecutive Parts without a literature check". If a threshold is exceeded, propose an audit rather than starting a new Part.

### The second type: detecting circles (injection → recovery)

Background: in the monograph layer early in the construction, it is recorded in §5 of the monograph, as a negative result, that attempts to approach the zeros and explicit formulas ended in "external injection, trivial realization, or circular reasoning". This is a failure of a different kind from horizontal convergence (the first type above) and needs separate detection.

Types of circle:

- **Injection → recovery**: putting in from outside the target quantity (or information equivalent to it) as an axiom, weight, phase, etc., and taking out its rereading as an "achievement". Example: reading the Kloosterman sums, which come out by classical identities from the powers of Gauss sums put in by axiom A1, as "generated by the system". Even if the precision increases, if it is the precision of the injected approximation itself, the system adds nothing.
- **Circularity through equivalence**: a condition that looks like progress is in fact equivalent to the target. Example (N7 in §5 of the monograph): the requirement of a self-adjoint realization for all phases was equivalent to the Hermite–Biehler property, essentially equivalent to the generalized Riemann hypothesis.
- **Trivial realization of a coincidence of form**: the output having "the same form" as the target follows trivially from the construction. Example: even if eigenvalues have the same form as Euler factors, they are not on the critical line and contain no information about zeros.

Detection procedure (carry out before presenting a new claim or achievement):

1. **Make a dependency table**: for each claim, list the inputs its conclusion depends on (axioms, injected weights and phases, theorems borrowed from outside). If the target quantity itself, or information equivalent to it, is on the input side, report it as a circle. The "list of external injections" in §5.2 of the monograph is an example of this device; make it before, not after, the achievement.
2. **Removal test**: remove one suspicious input and check whether the conclusion remains. If removing it makes the conclusion disappear or become empty, that input was carrying the content, and write "put in and reread", not "derived" (example: removing A1 makes $\alpha_\chi$ free again).
3. **Equivalence check and information audit**: check that the imposed condition is not equivalent to the target. When claiming a correspondence, compare the amounts of information (growth of counts, etc.), and do not claim the correspondence if the orders of magnitude do not match.

### Other types (types 3 to 11)

Examples and details are in `files/failure-patterns-audit.md`. Only the essentials are given here as operating rules. Only the first type needs a literature check; all of the following can be detected by internal checks.

- **Type 3: upgrading of claims**. Match superlatives and words of necessity, isomorphism and generation in prose against the formal statements of the corresponding theorems. A claim for which no formal statement can be written is downgraded or put under [observation]. Make the correction table a routine of each Part, not something "at the time of writing" (the monograph's correction table is an example). Added response (2026-11-13): write titles and claim statements from the formal statements after the Lean formal statements are settled (a response to Parts 255 and 256 having titles and claims broader than the formal statements two Parts in a row, which were narrowed by the checks).
- **Type 4: mis-setting of the question**. Before tackling a new proposition, first compute obstruction quantities (defect indices, invariants, dimension counts), and consider the opposite direction (counterexamples, non-uniqueness) once. The targeted proposition itself may be false (Step 3 of the origin document is an example).
- **Type 5: dropping boundary cases**. Numerical checks must include not only random general cases but also degenerate cases (r = 0, p = 2, the smallest modulus, the empty sum, the trivial character) (sgn(0) in Proposition 4.7 and p = 2 in Part 8 are examples).
- **Type 6: naive generalization**. Search for counterexamples before starting a proof. Keep a "counterexample" column permanently in the check table, and widen the search range when the words "always" or "necessarily" appear (design Parts 97 and 169 are examples).
- **Type 7: confusing numerical noise with counterexamples**. Before declaring a numerical discrepancy a counterexample, estimate the source of the error (condition number, cancellation, truncation) and reconfirm by another method. Even when judging "not a counterexample", leave the grounds in the memo (Parts 111, 115–116 and 125 are examples of practice).
- **Type 8: artifacts of conventions and normalizations**. Before presenting a claim, check that it survives changing normalizations and conventions. Do not report as a phenomenon a "phenomenon" that disappears when the convention changes (the Haar normalization of Part 197 is an example).
- **Type 9: process drift**. For files referred to, confirm the version and the numbering correspondence (beware of the offset between design numbers and Lean numbers, and of drafts coexisting with finished versions) (the authoritative copy is on the LEAN.zip side, and the correspondence follows the archive catalog).
- **Type 10: rhetoric of emergence**. To claims that something "appeared", "was generated" or "connected", always attach "which term of which theorem is new". If this cannot be written, separate it as [observation], not as an achievement. Since prompts asking about significance return words of significance, do not anticipate emergence on the question side either.
- **Type 11: drift of formal statements**. What sorry 0 and `#print axioms` guarantee is only that the proof shows the formal statement; they do not guarantee that the formal statement means the same as the intended claim. For each main theorem, before writing the note: construct a concrete example satisfying all hypotheses (a witness of non-vacuity); search for counterexamples with small parameters for the statement with each hypothesis removed one at a time (the formal-statement version of the removal test); give only the formal statement and the definitions to a separate session that is not shown the note, have it reverse-translate them into prose, and match that against the claim. Details are under type 11 in `failure-patterns-audit.md`.

**Independence of the detector (common to all types)**: detection concerning "content", "claims" and "novelty" (types 1, 2, 3, 10 and 11) is not left to self-audit by a session with the same context as the construction, but is done in a new session given only the object of inspection. The author's definitions (§1) and axiom A1 are not changed, but if signs of agreement with a known object, injection of target information, or trivialization of formal statements are found, do not change anything; report them to the author as "findings".

Where it operates (2026-11-09, the author's decision): routine checks are done when closing a Part, with the construction side's workflow starting a new agent that has no context. Two conditions apply: what is given to the inspecting side is only the formal statements and definitions, not the prose of the Part (explanations of what was achieved), and the request text is a fixed form; and the inspecting side is made to produce artifacts (witnesses of non-vacuity, counterexamples, reverse-translated statements), not judgments of "correct or not". Parts claiming new candidates and Parts with main-theorem-level claims are checked doubly, also in a separate chat on the author side (Muse). The record of a check includes the full text of the request to the inspecting side and the time taken (a response to the gap in the record where the request texts for A and C were not kept in Part 251). Wording corrected and items added after a check are re-checked before closing that Part (a response to the remaining item "post-check corrections unchecked" appearing in Parts 252 and 254, two Parts in a row; added 2026-11-13). When correcting wording because of a check, put the same correction into all the wording — the note, the table, and the Lean docstrings and opening explanation — and treat everything up to the check as one set (a rule because, in the re-check of Parts 252–256, all discrepancies were due to "post-check corrections not having been put into the docstrings and opening explanations"; added 2026-11-14).
